## Regression (p.9–39)
### Model forms
- p.9 "A Univariate linear Regression model: $Y=\beta_0+\beta\mathbf X+\varepsilon$"; "Y is often called **predictand**. It depend on (response to) **predictors X**"; $X_1,\dots,X_M$ "also called the independent or explanatory variables"; $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_M$ "are the regression parameters and ε is random error to account for random deviations from the linear relation"; M = number of predictors; "Y, X are observed values while β and ε are unobserved population quantities derived through regression fit"; "'**Univariate' refers to a single predictand, regardless of the number of predictors**"; multiple predictors (M>1) = "Univariate Multiple Linear Regression".
- p.10 quadratic: $Y=\beta_0+\beta_1X+\beta_2X^2+\varepsilon$ — "Y still depends linearly on the regression parameters β₁ and β₂. The fact that the predictors may be nonlinear functions of X is irrelevant." Figure: line $Y=-0.81X+4.05$ (R²=0.23) vs quadratic $Y=-0.98X+0.80X^2+1.85$ (R²=0.80).
- p.11 log-linear: $Y=\alpha\exp(\beta X+\varepsilon)$ → $\log Y=\log\alpha+\beta X+\varepsilon$ ("log α and β are the regression parameters"). Figure: $Y=3.62X-0.49$ (R²=0.82) vs "Poisson" $Y=2.18\exp(0.47X)$ (R²=0.92).
- p.12 least squares: $Y_n=X_{n1}\beta_1+\dots+X_{nM}\beta_M+\epsilon_n$; matrix $\mathbf y=\mathbf X\boldsymbol\beta+\boldsymbol\epsilon$ (N×1 = N×M · M×1 + N×1);
$$SSE=\sum_{n=1}^N(Y_n-\beta_1X_{n1}-\dots-\beta_MX_{nM})^2$$
  "The goal is to minimize the Sum of squared error (SSE)."
### R², adjusted R², CI
- p.13 "cave man" null model: best prediction without X is the mean $\hat\mu_y$; $SST=\sum(y_i-\hat\mu_y)^2$; "$R = 1-\frac{SSE}{SST}$ (sic, R²): How much better is your model than predicting a mean"; "SST is the total squared deviation from the 'null' model in the F-test, and tests your full model against this model."
- p.14 "The positive square root of R² is known as the **multiple correlation R**"; one predictor + intercept → R² = squared correlation between Y and X; "R² can be interpreted equivalently as the fraction of variance 'explained' by the predictors"; R = correlation between ŷ and y; R = maximum correlation between Y and any linear combination of X; "**Adjusted R²**: a modified version of R² that adjusts for the number of predictors".
- p.15 (viewed):
$$CI=\beta\pm t_{critical}\times SE(\beta);\quad SSE=\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\hat y_i)^2;\quad \hat\sigma^2=\frac{SSE}{n-(p+1)}$$
  "**Degrees of freedom = n − (number of predictors + 1)**". One predictor: $SE(\hat\beta_1)=\frac{\hat\sigma}{\sqrt{(X-\bar X)^2}}$ (sic, sum), df = n−2; "for trend analysis, we replace X with t". Multiple: $SE(\hat\beta)=\hat\sigma\sqrt{(X^TX)}$ (sic); "Python OLS function provides the SE for each predictor"; "Check if SE(β̂) and $t_{critical}$ should be inflated due to autocorrelation of the residuals".
- p.16 "it is important to always include an intercept": burned area with intercept $A=-40566+5009\,VPD$ (R²=0.63) vs without $A=543\,VPD$ (R²=0.13).

### Model selection (p.24–30)
- p.24 choosing predictors: number of annual harmonics; order of AR model; number of predictors; "Model selection is not easily addressed using a hypothesis testing framework because **multiple testing** is involved."
- p.25 "**Forward selection** builds a regression model incrementally, starting from the intercept-only model, at each step, adding the predictor that gives the greatest improvement in fit, stopping when no remaining predictor improves the model by a meaningful amount." "**Backward elimination** starts from the full model containing all candidate predictors and, at each step, removes the predictor whose deletion least degrades the fit, stopping when removing any further predictor would worsen the model by a meaningful amount."
- p.26 forward procedure, 5 candidate predictors: (1) $Y=\beta_o+\beta_aX_a$, fit 5 models, pick smallest MSE; (2) $Y=\beta_o+\beta_aX_a+\beta_bX_b$, a fixed, fit 4 models … Correlations of Y with X1…X5: 0.36, −0.42, 0.24, 0.71, 0.19.
- p.27 results: forward picks X4 first (in-sample RMSE ~5.8), then X2, then X3; "X4 is always included"; "X4 & X2 are always included"; backward eliminates X5 then X1; "We don't need to include X1 & X5".
- p.28 pairwise predictor correlations: "X1 and X4 are strongly correlated" (r=0.35; others |r| ≤ 0.21) — explains why X1 adds nothing once X4 is in.
- p.29 "In forward selection, adding a predictor can only keep the training R² the same or increase it, even if the predictor is random noise; a higher training R² therefore does not mean the new predictor is useful." "Removing a predictor from a regression model always keeps the training R² the same or makes it lower, even if the removed predictor is random noise." R² path: x4 ≈0.50, +x2 ≈0.80, +x3 ≈0.87, then flat.
- p.30 John von Neumann: "With four parameters I can fit an elephant, and with five I can make him wiggle his trunk."
### Evaluation & cross-validation (p.31–36)
- p.31 "A general idea for model evaluation is to see how well a model predicts '**out-of-sample**' data." Fit Y on X2, X3, X4 using the first 50 points: $\hat Y=11.05-3.04X_2+0.145X_3+2.09X_4$; R²=0.89, RMSE=2.76. Apply to the last 10 points: **R²=0.60, RMSE=3.89** — "This is what tells you how good a model is!" (All-60-point fit: $\hat Y=11.12-2.851X_2+0.141X_3+2.011X_4$; R²=0.873; RMSE=2.96.)
- p.32 CV: "withhold some samples from the fitting process, fit the model to the remaining sample, then use the resulting model to predict the withheld sample"; training sample vs testing/validation sample; "forward selection is often conducted with CV, but the CV should use the **training data ONLY** (you don't want your predictor selection procedure to be influenced by the testing data)"; **LOOCV**: leave one of N out, fit on N−1; "The RMSE of the LOOCV is the RMSE of the M (sic, N) out-of-sample prediction error".
- p.33 **K-fold**: split into K roughly equal parts; withhold kth, fit on K−1; repeat k=1…K → a prediction for each of N samples;
$$MSE_{CV}=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^Ne_n^2$$
  Figure (first 50 samples): training RMSE, LOOCV RMSE, test RMSE (samples 51–60), 5-fold CV RMSE (mean ± sd, 100 repetitions); CV RMSE minimum around 3 predictors (x4, x2, x3), rises slightly after.
- p.34 no overfitting: y ~ x2+x3+x4, repeated 5-fold CV (200 repetitions × 5 folds = 1000 fits): CV RMSE 3.02 (SD 0.08); training RMSE mean 2.72 (SD 0.16); validation RMSE mean 2.95 (SD 0.65); true noise σ=3.

- p.36 random-split CV (1000 splits, 40/10) vs LOOCV vs test: "A gap between test and in-sample RMSE always exists; if it is small, the model is acceptable"; "All these approaches lead to similar answers in our example"; "Why repeat cross-validation many times?" ## Detection & Attribution (p.40–52)
- p.41 "Separatory (sic) climate variability to two additive parts: forced and unforced variability". **Forced**: "the response of the climate system to external mechanisms, such as changes in solar insolation, atmospheric composition as a result of volcanic activity and human-caused emissions, land-use practices, etc." **Unforced (noise)**: "internal variability, which arises naturally due to atmospheric instability and from atmosphere-ocean-ice-land interactions".
- p.42 $\mathbf O=\mathbf f+\boldsymbol\varepsilon$: O = observed response (single series e.g. global mean temperature, or a vector of series from a network); f forced; ε unforced noise; often consider increasing GHG and aerosol impact.
- p.43
$$\mathbf o=\beta_{GHG}\mathbf f_{GHG}+\beta_{AER}\mathbf f_{AER}+\beta_{NAT}\mathbf f_{NAT}+\varepsilon$$
  f's and ε "come from models, whether physical, statistical, or hybrid"; "Models are not perfect. A standard approach is to allow that the scale or amplitude of the response may be wrong." "**Scaling factors** … indicate how much each modeled response has to be stretched or shrunk to match the observations." "The value of β_i … informs us about the quality of the hypothesized forced response."
  - **Detected**: "The hypothesis β = 0 is rejected (the CI excludes 0)."
  - **Attributed (consistent)**: "detected, and β = 1 cannot be rejected (the CI also includes 1): the observed response has the same pattern and the same amplitude as the model predicts. The model's explanation of the observed change is then quantitatively consistent with the data."
  - Figure: (a) detection — control-run noise centred on β=0, observed β=0.58 outside → GHG DETECTED; (b) attribution — noise centred on β=1, observed inside → CONSISTENT.
- p.44 "If β_i > 1, then the hypothesized forcing f_i **underestimates** the magnitude of the ith forced response." "β_i < 1 implies the hypothesized forcing f_i **overestimates** the magnitude." "The hypothesis test can be assessed using the confidence interval." DelSole & Tippett Fig 9.5 categories: "detected and attributed" (CI excludes 0, includes 1); "not detected and not consistent" (CI includes 0, excludes 1); "detected but underestimated" (CI entirely above 1); "consistent but not detected" (CI includes both 0 and 1).
- p.45 GCM global temperature example (synthetic, 1950–2017, 50-member ensembles; obs proxy = member 1; f's = mean of members 2–50; control run 996 yr):
$$O=\beta_0+\beta_{GHG}F_{GHG}+\beta_{AER}F_{AER}+\beta_{NAT}F_{NAT}=\beta_0+\beta_{GHG}(F_{all}-F_{AER}-F_{AER})+\beta_{AER}F_{AER}+\beta_{NAT}F_{NAT}$$
  (sic — second $F_{AER}$ should be $F_{NAT}$; GHG response derived as all-forcing minus aerosol minus natural). Steps: get X and Y; fit β; check CI — σ, ACF(noise), effective N, inflated SE and $t_{critical}$; detected & attributed?; attributable amount? Control-run AR(1) fit $r_1=0.41$.
